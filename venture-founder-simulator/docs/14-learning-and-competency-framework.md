# 14. Learning and Competency Framework

## 1. Teaching model

Learn by deciding. Concepts appear when they matter, explained with the player's own numbers, at three depths:

| Depth | Format | Length |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | Inline tooltip: plain definition + the player's current value | 1 to 2 lines |
| L2 | "Explain this" drawer: definition, formula, worked example on player data, why it matters, how to read it, common mistakes, benchmark (only if sourced), what decision it informs | 1 screen |
| L3 | Concept card, worked example, case link, practice exercise, knowledge check | 5 to 15 minutes |

No modal lectures. L3 opens only on request, or once as a suggestion after a related mistake.

### 1.1 "Explain this" example (generated deterministically, AI may rephrase)

> **Contribution margin (delivery channel): 31%**
> For every ₹100 of delivery sales last month, ₹31 was left after food cost (₹33), aggregator commission (₹25), GST on commission you cannot claim (₹4.5), packaging (₹4) and discounts you funded (₹2.5).
> Formula: (Revenue - variable costs) / Revenue.
> Why it matters: your fixed costs are ₹4.2 lakh a month. At 31%, delivery needs ₹13.5 lakh of sales a month to cover them alone.
> Common mistake: comparing this to dine-in without allocating packaging and platform ads.
> Decision it informs: whether to push delivery, raise delivery prices, or limit delivery menu.

(Illustrative values. In-product values come from the ledger.)

## 4. Levels

| Level | Name | Evidence that proves it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduced | Viewed concept; completed L1/L2 |
| 2 | Understands | Passed knowledge check (≥ 80%) |
| 3 | Can calculate or analyse | Correct calculation on own data or exercise (auto-checked) |
| 4 | Can make a reasoned decision | Decision with rationale meeting rubric (assumptions stated, alternatives considered, evidence cited) |
| 5 | Can execute in the simulation | Decision executed and outcome tracked, with variance explained |
| 6 | Can adapt to new scenarios | Correct adaptation when an event or new evidence invalidates a prior plan, in a different context from where it was learned |

A level is awarded only with linked evidence. The report shows the evidence, not just the badge.

## 5. Judging decisions, not results

Outcome alone is a poor measure: a good decision can turn out badly through bad luck, and a reckless one can get lucky. The assessment separates:

| Dimension | How measured |
|---|---|
| Process quality | Rubric on rationale: stated assumptions, evidence used, alternatives, risk considered, reversibility considered |
| Information use | Did the player use available evidence (e.g. ran footfall counts before lease)? Gate overrides count against this unless justified |
| Outcome | Financial and operational results |
| Luck adjustment | Re-run the same decisions under N alternate seeds (e.g. 20). Report the outcome distribution. A result in the bottom decile of its own distribution is flagged as bad luck, not bad judgement, and vice versa |

The counterfactual seed re-run is a strong teaching tool for debriefs and is cheap because the engine is deterministic.

## 6. Rubric example (C07 Cash discipline, decision level)

| Score | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 1 | No cash forecast used; shortfall discovered on the day |
| 2 | Forecast exists but not updated; buffer not set |
| 3 | Weekly forecast maintained; shortfalls spotted; reactive levers used |
| 4 | Shortfall anticipated 3+ weeks ahead; lever chosen with cost comparison |
| 5 | Buffer policy set and kept; forecast accuracy improving; financing arranged before need |

## 7. Founder readiness report

Sections: demonstrated strengths (with evidence links) · weak areas · decisions made well · costly mistakes (with ₹ impact and what would have avoided them) · concepts understood (level per competency) · **practical tasks still needing real-world exposure** (always present, e.g. "negotiating a real lease", "hiring and managing a real team", "real GST filing with a professional") · recommended next learning and fieldwork.

The report must not state or imply real-world mastery. Standard wording: "This report shows what you did in a simulation. It is evidence of reasoning and practice, not proof that you can run a real business."

Optional diagnostic at start and end (same competencies, parallel forms) so institutions can measure learning gain.
